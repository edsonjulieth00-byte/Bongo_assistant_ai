# Tanzania Residence Permits

Source authority: Tanzania Immigration Services Department
Official source:
- https://eservices.immigration.go.tz/online/web/permit/guidelines
- https://www.immigration.go.tz/index.php/regulations

## Overview

Foreigners intending to reside in Tanzania for investment, business, employment or another legal activity may be issued a Residence Permit.

The official Immigration guidance identifies three broad classes:
- Class A — self-employed foreigners/investors.
- Class B — expatriates employed by companies/private institutions and qualifying employers.
- Class C — other categories such as researchers, retired persons, missionaries, volunteers and others.

Foreigners intending to reside in Tanzania for more than 90 days for the stated purposes generally require a residence permit.

## Online application

The official e-Residence Permit guidance states that applicants should:
1. Visit the official e-Residence Permit service.
2. Read the Residence Permit Guidelines.
3. Create an account.
4. Complete the online application.
5. Attach the required documents.
6. Submit the application.
7. If approved for payment, pay the prescribed fee within the stated period.
8. Receive an enrolment notification after payment.
9. Report to the appropriate Immigration Office for fingerprints and photograph.
10. Complete enrolment and await processing/issuance.

The current online guidance states that approved applications must be paid within 60 days; otherwise the application is automatically deleted.

## Work permit relationship

The official guidance states that applicants generally need the relevant Work Permit before a Residence Permit, except for categories such as studies, research, court cases and retirement where different rules apply.

Applications requiring a Work Permit may be handled through the relevant labour/online system as directed by the official guidance.

## Class A

Class A is for self-employed foreign investors and covers sectors such as:
- Mining and prospecting.
- Agriculture and animal husbandry.
- Professional and consultancy services.
- Professional arts.
- Manufacturing and processing.
- Large-scale investment and trade.
- Hospitality.
- Information technology.
- Transport.
- Construction.
- Real estate.
- Other eligible investments.

General Class A requirements listed by Immigration include:
- Completed online application.
- Application letter.
- Work Permit where required.
- Passport valid for at least six months.
- Passport-size photograph.
- Full-size photograph.
- Relevant registration certificates.
- Investment certificate where applicable.
- Company/director documents where applicable.
- Certificate of incorporation/compliance/business registration as applicable.
- Valid business licence.
- TIN registration.
- Tax clearance.
- Tax payment evidence.
- Lease agreement or Certificate of Occupancy.
- Company asset list.
- Other documents where required.

## Class B

Class B is for foreigners with qualifying skills/qualifications employed by a specific employer, particularly occupations where the required skills are not readily available locally.

General requirements include:
- Completed online application.
- Application letter.
- Work Permit.
- Passport valid for at least six months.
- Employer covering letter.
- Passport and full-size photographs.
- CV.
- Certified academic certificates.
- Official translations where required.
- Job description.
- Employment contract.
- Previous permit for renewal/replacement where applicable.
- Organization structure.
- Relevant professional registration/certification where required.
- Business and company documents.
- Tax documents.

## Class C

Class C covers eligible foreigners who do not fall under Class A or B, including categories such as:
- Researchers.
- Retired persons.
- Missionaries.
- Volunteers.
- Persons receiving medical treatment.
- NGO employees.
- Interns.
- Persons attending court cases.
- Professional artists.
- Other eligible categories.

Requirements depend on the specific Class C category. Examples in the official guidance include research permits, institutional letters, volunteer contracts, evidence of retirement, medical evidence, court evidence and academic documents.

## Residence permit status categories

The official guidance identifies application statuses including:
- First Grant (FG).
- Returnee (RT).
- Second Grant (SG).
- Replacement (RPL).
- Re-Grant 1 (RG1).
- Re-Grant 2 (RG2).

## Important chatbot rule

Residence permit requirements vary significantly by class, occupation, nationality/status and application type. The chatbot must ask for the relevant category when necessary and must not present one class's requirements as requirements for all applicants.
